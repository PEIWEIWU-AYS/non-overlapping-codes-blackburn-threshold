A quadratic alphabet threshold for non-overlapping codes

Peiwei Wu1 and Qian Peng2

1 Shandong Medical and Pharmaceutical University (formerly Binzhou Medical University)
2 The University of Melbourne · Corresponding author

Abstract

Let q₀(n) be the least integer such that every alphabet of size q ≥ q₀(n) admits a partition I ⊔ J for which Iⁿ⁻¹J is a maximum non-overlapping code of length n. Chee proved that q₀(n) exists. We prove q₀(n) = n²/2 + O(n) and the finite bound q₀(n) ≤ 4n(n−1). The upper bound compares a uniform loss in the real partition relaxation with the cost of rounding a single alphabet split. Moving one two-letter word between parts produces an infinite family of exceptions and disproves a conjecture of Stanovnik, Moškon and Mraz. We determine the maximum for every alphabet size at lengths five and six. Their thresholds are 4 and 10, respectively. At length six the exceptional alphabet sizes are 2, 3, 4, 9. On nine symbols the maximum is 33,872, exceeding the best I⁵J code by 258.
