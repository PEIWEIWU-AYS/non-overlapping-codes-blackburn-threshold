# Literature and independent checks for the alphabet threshold

Public records were searched on 27–28 November 2026, Melbourne time. The questions checked were the growth of the least threshold in Chee's theorem, the resulting counterexamples to the conjecture of Stanovnik, Moškon and Mraz, and the maximum size at length five over every alphabet.

## Precedents and the remaining questions

Blackburn determined the maximum for lengths up to three and proposed eventual optimality of the codes \(I^{n-1}J\) for fixed length and growing alphabet. Stanovnik, Moškon and Mraz determined the maximum for length four. Their Section 6 reports integer computations at lengths five and above for alphabets of up to six symbols. Their Conjecture 1 restricts the nonempty right parts of at least one optimal partition. Remark 3 records its consequence that a Blackburn code is maximum whenever \(q\ge n\). [Blackburn](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.1026) · [Stanovnik, Moškon and Mraz, Theorem 19 and Section 6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-023-01344-z)

Chee's Theorem I.1 proves eventual optimality for every fixed length. Section IV explicitly asks for the least threshold \(q_0(n)\) and all exceptional pairs. Theorem III.2 handles partitions with a sufficiently unequal first split. Lemma III.3 uses a compactness argument for the remaining partitions. Blackburn's November 2026 revision settles the leading upper-bound constant with the alphabet size fixed and the length growing. The two limits have different implications for finite exceptions. [Chee, Section IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.28776v2#S4) · [Blackburn, revised fixed-alphabet bound](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21913v2)

## Provenance of the witness family

The witness uses the nested-partition construction of Fimmel and coauthors, subsequently analyzed by Stanovnik, Moškon and Mraz. Even the particular pattern with one word on the left at level two and empty higher left parts has a precedent. The published authors' computation for \((q,n)=(3,5)\) lists left counts \((1,1,3,7)\) and right counts \((2,1,0,0)\). Exchanging the two sides gives left counts \((2,1,0,0)\). Its reported maximum of 17 is the length-five exception in the present formula. Their tabulation also covers the binary value 2. The contributions examined here are the parameter choices refuting Conjecture 1, the order of the last exceptional alphabet, and the length-five classification across all alphabet sizes. [Authors' source table](https://github.com/magdevska/nono-codes/blob/d71b2a10c764bdd86b3a2754bf819c20927a3d18/consistency/compute_sqn-serial.txt) · [Authors' full supplemental tables](https://github.com/magdevska/nono-codes/blob/d71b2a10c764bdd86b3a2754bf819c20927a3d18/results/optimal_solutions.pdf) · [Partition-code definition and history](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.28776v2#S2.SS2)

For the nine-symbol, length-six witness let \(I=\{0,\dots,6\}\) and \(J=\{7,8\}\). The first letter lies in \(I\), the last in \(J\), every interior occurrence from \(J\) is a 7 immediately following a 0, and the whole word cannot end in 07. The projection of this code onto its first position has size 7, its last position has size 2, and every interior position has projection size 8.

These projections distinguish this witness from Blackburn's marker construction (Construction I.A in Wang–Wang), including its reversal and alphabet relabelings. In every nonempty instance of that construction, the last-position projection is the whole marker part. For \(k<5\), position \(k+1\) is restricted to the marker part. An interior projection of size 8 exceeds both end projection sizes of the witness. For \(k=5\), the first five positions must use the complement of the marker part, whose size for this witness would be 7 in the forward orientation and 2 in the reversed orientation. Wang and Wang's lift of binary pattern codes to two symbol classes permits free substitution inside each class at each position. A first-position projection of size 7 forces the partition into the seven initial symbols and the two others. The words 070008 and 170008 have the same class pattern and different membership in the witness. This tests the specific named constructions and leaves the attribution to the general nested-partition construction above intact. [Wang and Wang, Section III and Construction I.A](https://arxiv.org/html/2108.06934v1#S3)

## Exact six-letter check

A direct enumeration tested all \(9^6=531{,}441\) words against the four conditions above and retained **33,851**. Comparing the prefix and suffix sets of the retained words gave the following counts.

| Length | Distinct prefixes | Distinct suffixes | Intersection size |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 7 | 2 | 0 |
| 2 | 50 | 15 | 0 |
| 3 | 357 | 106 | 0 |
| 4 | 2,549 | 757 | 0 |
| 5 | 18,200 | 5,405 | 0 |

The same size follows from the level sizes \(2,13,93,664,4{,}741,33{,}851\). The best Blackburn size is \(\max_{1\le s<9}s^5(9-s)=33{,}614\), achieved at \(s=7\). Thus this witness exceeds the best Blackburn size by 237. This comparison establishes a counterexample independently of determining the exact value of \(S_6(9)\).

The public evidence examined contained no earlier proof of the quadratic threshold order, a matching quadratic upper or lower threshold bound, the stated counterexample to the 2024 conjecture, or the length-five formula over all alphabet sizes. Different terminology, indexing delays and unpublished work limit this finding.

A further targeted search combined the two code names with Blackburn, the least alphabet threshold, quadratic growth, the constant one half, and the linear remainder. It returned Chee’s fixed-length existence theorem and work on the fixed-alphabet regime. We did not locate the formula q₀(n) = n²/2 + O(n) in these public records.
